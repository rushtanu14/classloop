# ClassLoop Beta Outreach Kit

Last updated: 2026-06-01

Use this when asking teachers, tutors, club leads, or workshop facilitators to try ClassLoop. Keep the ask small, honest, and privacy-safe: 15 minutes, sample or anonymized data, and feedback on what is wrong or useful.

## Outreach Rule

Every outreach message should answer four things:

- What ClassLoop does: turns messy class records into teacher-reviewed student follow-ups.
- Why it matters: saves the after-class cleanup work.
- What the tester has to do: try one sample or anonymized session for about 15 minutes.
- What is safe: no raw student records in public demos; real data stays desktop or reviewed pilot only.

## Short DM

Hey, I am building ClassLoop, a teacher assistant for after-class follow-up. It turns transcripts, notes, rosters, and links into a teacher-reviewed recap, student next steps, resources, and completion check-ins.

Would you be open to trying it for about 15 minutes with sample or anonymized class/tutoring notes? I am mostly looking for what is wrong, confusing, or actually useful before I invite more people.

No real student records needed.

## Confirmation Message

Thank you. For the test, please use sample or anonymized data first.

Suggested path:

1. Open the hosted demo or desktop app.
2. Try one transcript/notes + roster/resources flow.
3. Reach publish preview if possible.
4. Check one student preview.
5. Fill out the beta feedback form or send notes on what was wrong, confusing, or useful.

Do not paste raw student records, grades, passwords, or private notes into public feedback.

## Thank-You Message

Thank you for testing ClassLoop. The most useful feedback is what felt wrong, unclear, risky, or slow. If you would use it again with another session, that is the strongest signal; if not, the reason is just as useful.

## Feedback Summary Request

Could you send quick answers to these?

1. Did you reach publish preview?
2. What was the most useful generated part?
3. What was wrong or confusing?
4. What would you need before students could see it?
5. Would this save time after class or tutoring?
